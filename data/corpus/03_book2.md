# The Kesh Ithar Accord

> Book Two opens in Hollow of 1144, eight months after the *Long Patience* reached the Glass
> Bazaar. Several things that were true in Book One stop being true in this book. Each is dated
> where it changes.

## Chapter 1 — The Master of the Long Patience

In Hollow of 1144, at Kesh Ithar, **Oren Dask retired**.

He was fifty-nine and had made twenty-two passages of the Line, and he gave two reasons, of which
only the second was true. The first was his knees. The second was Attick Sell, who had died at the
twenty-first stone in Emberwane of 1143 because Dask had decided, correctly by arithmetic and
wrongly by every other measure, that the Compact would break before he did.

He signed the licence of the *Long Patience* over to **Sera Volant** in the same month.

So from Hollow of 1144 onward the caravan master of the *Long Patience* is Sera Volant, who was
twenty-four in that month and turned twenty-five in Thaw of 1144 — the youngest master licensed on the Line in the Concord's records. Oren Dask holds no
office in this book. He keeps a room above a glass-grinder's shop in the Bazaar and is consulted
more often than he is paid.

## Chapter 2 — The Fires at the Spur

In Greening of 1144 the **Lamplighters' Guild reopened the Thennic Spur**.

The clearing had taken the Guild two seasons and a great deal of other people's money. What matters
is not the rock but the fires: the Guild relit the signal fire at the fourteenth stone and the fire
at the twenty-second, and by the only convention that has ever governed the Line, that made the
Spur a road again.

The Thennic Spur has therefore been **open since Greening 1144**. Caravans may take it. Nine did in
its first season, and eight of them arrived.

Sera Volant did not take it. "It saves eight stones of salt," she said, "and it costs you Hesh's
cord and the Gate's cistern, and on a bad passage those are the two things that keep you alive."

## Chapter 3 — The Accord

In Longlight of 1144 the Harbour Syndicate and the Nine Tables signed the **Kesh Ithar Accord**,
and the Waystone Tithe changed for the first time in ninety-one years.

Under the Accord, and in place of the old schedule:

- the tithe is **one tenth-mark** per wagon for each waystone the wagon passes, counting both the
  first waystone and the last; and
- it is levied **only on the waystones north of Ossuary Gate** — that is, on waystones one to
  sixteen. **No tithe is owed on waystones seventeen to forty.**

Both halves of that change matter, and the second is the one the Tables fought for. Halving the
rate cost the Reach money. Confining the tithe to the northern half cost the Reach the Flats, which
it had been taxing for ninety years and policing for none of them.

Tulvo Brann, who held the fourth seat and did most of the drafting, was asked afterwards what the
Accord had cost Kesh Ithar. "A great deal less than it cost Halvern Reach," he said, "which is not
the same as saying it was cheap."

The old rate — two tenth-marks per wagon per waystone, levied the whole length of the Line — stands
in the Syndicate's schedule as the rate for every passage up to and including Longlight 1144.

## Chapter 4 — Mirrowmere

In Dustfall of 1144 the water at **Mirrowmere** dropped early, as it does perhaps once in nine
years out of season, and the roofs of Old Mirrow came up out of the lake, and Sera Volant went east
off the Line to see them, because her mother had surveyed them and the survey had never been found.

**Marrowen Vehl** was there, sitting on a drowned wall with his boots in the water.

What he told her, and what the rest of the book is about, is this: **Ravenna Volant was his sister**.
He had been born at Varro's Ford, had gone into the Flats at nineteen, and had not spoken to Ravenna
in the six years before the Vessel came up in 1131. **Marrowen Vehl is Sera Volant's uncle.**

He had recognised the astrolabe at the twenty-first stone in Emberwane of 1143. He had been looking
at it for six days across a cistern before she rode out to him with it on her belt, and had opened
the cisterns for it, and had let a drover die first because the Compact's people were watching and
he had four thousand of them to answer to and one niece.

Sera did not forgive him for that in this book.

## Chapter 5 — The Quiet Ledger, in daylight

Also in Dustfall of 1144, and partly because of Mirrowmere, the Quiet Ledger stopped being secret
in any useful sense.

**Ilsa Mirren**, Keeper of the Tide Clock at Halvern Reach, confirmed in writing to the Syndic that
she had been a **member of the Quiet Ledger** for nineteen years, which is to say for the whole of
her keepership and eight years before it. She had been Ravenna Volant's correspondent and, after
1131, the keeper of what survived of her surveys.

The Syndic did not prosecute her. The Accord had made the Ledger's great grievance — a tithe levied
on stones the Syndicate had never surveyed — into somebody else's problem, and a secret society
without a grievance is a correspondence club.

## Chapter 6 — Frostmark 1144

Two offices changed hands in Frostmark of 1144.

**Ilsa Mirren stood down as Keeper of the Tide Clock**, after twelve years, and the Syndic appointed
**Pell Varrow** in her place. Pell was seventeen. No Keeper has ever been appointed younger, and the
appointment is generally read as Halbrecht Oye's apology to the Line for a sentence he had said in a
courtyard in Hollow of 1143 and had been quoted on ever since.

Pell has not opened the fourth dial. Pell winds it, as the Keeper is permitted to, and has written
down what winding it sounds like, which is the most that has ever been done about it.

And at **Ossuary Gate** the Lamplighters' Guild established a permanent station: **nine Guild
members**, quartered under the arch, from Frostmark of 1144. The Guild gave the reopening of the
Spur as its reason — two live routes through the Flats need two sets of fires and somebody in the
middle — and Hesh, who had tended the Gate alone for thirty-one seasons and now had nine colleagues,
tied a double knot in that season's cord, which in the cords means *a party arrived short of what it
set out with*, and would not be drawn on what he meant by it.

So from Frostmark 1144, Ossuary Gate has a permanent population of nine, and a tender who makes ten.
