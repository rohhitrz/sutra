# Factions and Institutions of the Meridian Line

Five bodies matter on the Line. Two govern cities, one governs the Flats, one keeps the fires, and
one keeps records it is not supposed to have.

---

## The Harbour Syndicate

Governing body of Halvern Reach, under a Harbour Syndic. The Syndic in 1143 and 1144 is Halbrecht
Oye.

The Syndicate's power rests on one thing: it levies the **Waystone Tithe**, and has since 1053.

### The Waystone Tithe — the schedule of 1053

> The tithe is **two tenth-marks of silver, per wagon, for each waystone the wagon passes, counting
> both the first waystone and the last**. It is levied on the whole length of the Line.

A silver mark is ten tenth-marks. The schedule of 1053 stood unaltered for ninety-one years and
applies to **every passage up to and including Longlight 1144**, the standoff year of 1143 among
them.

The count of wagons is taken beneath the first waystone, in the Syndic's courtyard at Halvern Reach,
by a clerk of the tithe office, and that count is the Reach's claim on the caravan for the season.

### The Waystone Tithe — the Kesh Ithar Accord, Longlight 1144

> From Longlight 1144 the tithe is **one tenth-mark per wagon, for each waystone the wagon passes,
> counting both the first waystone and the last**, and is levied **only on the waystones north of
> Ossuary Gate — that is, on waystones one to sixteen**. No tithe is owed on waystones seventeen to
> forty.

Both halves of the change are load-bearing. Halving the rate cost the Reach money; confining the
tithe to the northern half cost it the Flats, which it had taxed for ninety-one years and policed
for none of them.

### Why the counting rule is written out twice

Because it is the only rule on the Line that people get wrong in both directions. A wagon that
starts at waystone one and stops at waystone seventeen has **passed seventeen waystones**, not
sixteen. The schedule says *counting both the first and the last*, and it means it.

## The Nine Tables

Governing council of Kesh Ithar. **Nine merchant seats**, fixed at nine in 1112, held for life, one
vote each.

The **fourth seat** is held by **Tulvo Brann**, who dealt in glass, salt, brass and carriage, and who
did most of the drafting of the Kesh Ithar Accord in Longlight 1144.

The Tables' long objection to the schedule of 1053 was not the rate but the geography: the tithe was
levied on waystones seventeen to forty, which the Reach neither surveyed nor protected, and the
goods on those wagons were Kesh Ithar's. The Accord is that objection, won.

## The Ashen Compact

The people of the Ashen Flats — perhaps four thousand of them, between the twelfth and twenty-fourth
waystones. Led in 1143 and 1144 by **Marrowen Vehl**, called the Cartwright.

Their arrangement has not changed in living memory and is not complicated: the cisterns on their
ground are theirs, and a caravan that wants to drink pays a **water toll**, in silver or in kind, at
a rate the Compact sets each season and will not negotiate at the cistern's edge.

The Compact is not a faction of the Concord and sends no representative to either city. The Reach's
broadsheets call its people bandits. The distinction the Compact draws — that a toll announced in
advance at a fixed rate is not a robbery — is the distinction the Reach has declined to accept for
ninety years, for reasons that are easier to understand once you have read the schedule of 1053.

## The Lamplighters' Guild

Chartered in Frostmark 1087. Keeps a fire-pan at every one of the forty waystones, and is paid by
**both** terminus cities, which is the whole of its claim to neutrality.

The Guild holds **the sole right to declare a closed route open**, which it exercises by relighting
the route's fires. This is not a formality and it is not symbolic: a route whose fires are out is a
route that is shut, and no licensed caravan may use it. It is the reason the Thennic Spur was shut
from Frostmark 1139 and the reason it was a road again in Greening 1144 — the Guild cleared the fall,
but it was the fires that reopened the pass.

The Guild established a permanent station of **nine** members at Ossuary Gate in Frostmark 1144.

## The Quiet Ledger

A society of cartographers and clerks, of no fixed membership and no premises, which keeps its own
survey of the true positions of the forty waystones.

Its grievance was precise. The schedule of 1053 charges by waystones passed; the Syndicate's own
survey of the stones is incomplete; therefore the Reach has for ninety-one years billed caravans
against a map it could not produce. The Ledger's answer was to produce one and not publish it.

**Recorded members:**

- **Ravenna Volant**, cartographer, died Emberwane 1131. Her surveys of the Vessel crossing and of
  Old Mirrow were the Ledger's most valuable and were not recovered at her death.
- **Ilsa Mirren**, Keeper of the Tide Clock at Halvern Reach, a member for nineteen years, which she
  declared in writing in Dustfall 1144. After 1131 she held what survived of Ravenna Volant's work.

Membership is not recorded in the Registry of Persons, which holds declared affiliations only. The
Kesh Ithar Accord took the Ledger's grievance away by taking the northern tithe down and the
southern tithe off, and a secret society without a grievance is a correspondence club.
