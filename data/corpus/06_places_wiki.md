# Places of the Meridian Line

The Line is a chain of **forty numbered waystones** crossing the continent of Sarrakand from north
to south. Waystone one stands at Halvern Reach; waystone forty stands at Kesh Ithar. The stones are
not evenly spaced. Each entry below gives the waystone number where there is one.

**Compiled Frostmark 1143. Amended through Frostmark 1144.** Amendments are dated in place; where
an entry carries both a Book One state and a dated amendment, **the dated amendment is the later
fact** and supersedes the line above it.

---

## Halvern Reach — waystone 1

Northern terminus of the Line. The city is cut back into basalt cliffs in seven terraces.

- **Population:** 41,000
- **Government:** the Harbour Syndicate, under a Harbour Syndic. The Syndic in 1143 and 1144 is
  Halbrecht Oye.
- **Waystone:** the first stone stands in the Syndic's courtyard. The Waystone Tithe is reckoned
  from the count of wagons taken beneath it.
- **Of note:** the **Tide Clock**, a water clock with four dials, on the lowest terrace in a hall
  open to the weather on three sides.

### The Tide Clock

Four dials. The **first** counts the tides, which at the Reach run twice in a day and a little over.
The **second** counts the hours, twenty to the day in the Reach's reckoning. The **third** is the
dial of the Line: a brass ring, one turn to the year, marked at forty points, which gives the length
of usable light at each waystone by season. The **fourth** dial is set behind a brass plate which
has not been opened in the Clock's recorded keeping. The Keeper winds it and is not told what it
counts; the article records this and goes no further, because nothing further is known.

Keepers: Ilsa Mirren, 1132 to Frostmark 1144. Pell Varrow, from Frostmark 1144.

## Varro's Ford — waystone 9

The Line meets the **Vessel River** at the ninth waystone. A shelf of flat rock lies a little under
two feet below the water in an ordinary season, and it is **the only place in the Vessel's whole
length where a loaded wagon can cross**. The town grew on the shelf.

- **Population:** 2,300 (1143)
- **Trade:** crossing fees, axles, rope, and the recovery of other people's cargo
- **Of note:** the river is not crossable in roughly four months of the eight. The Harbour Syndicate
  has never succeeded in stationing a tithe clerk at the Ford, for the reason that the taxable thing
  is underwater half the year.
- **1131:** the Vessel flooded in Emberwane. The cartographer Ravenna Volant drowned in it.

## The Ashen Flats — waystones 12 to 24

The salt desert at the centre of Sarrakand. The Line crosses it from the twelfth waystone to the
twenty-fourth.

There is no water in the Flats that a caravan has not carried in or paid for. The cisterns on the
Flats are held by the **Ashen Compact**, who levy a water toll on them at a rate set each season.
Perhaps four thousand people live in the Flats. The Reach's broadsheets call them bandits; the
Compact calls the arrangement a toll, and has not changed it in living memory.

## Thennic Spur — leaves the Line at waystone 14, rejoins at waystone 22

A mountain track that bypasses eight waystones of the Flats. Caravans used it for three centuries.

- **Frostmark 1139:** the shoulder above the narrows came down and filled the pass for a hundred
  yards with rock the size of wagons. The Lamplighters' Guild let the fires at the fourteenth and
  twenty-second waystones go out, **closing the route**.
- **Status in Book One (1143): closed.**
- **Greening 1144:** the Guild finished clearing the fall and **relit both fires**, which by the
  only convention that has ever governed the Line made the Spur a road again. It has been **open
  since Greening 1144**.
- Taking the Spur saves about twelve days and eight waystones. It also costs a caravan the cistern
  and the cord at Ossuary Gate, which is the argument against it.

## Ossuary Gate — waystone 17

A broken arch of the same stone as the waystones, standing over the seventeenth stone. There are no
bones in it and never have been; the name is older than any explanation of it.

The Gate is **the midpoint of the Line**. Seventeen is not the middle of forty, but the stones are
not evenly spaced, and the Gate is the middle by distance and by water, which are the two measures
that matter to a caravan.

- **Fixtures:** a cistern, a wind-break, a Lamplighters' fire-pan on a pole, and a tender.
- **Tender:** **Hesh**, mute, who keeps the Gate's records in knotted cord — one yard to the season,
  knots for wagons, spacing for days, a double knot for a party that arrived short of what it set out
  with. Thirty-one seasons of cord, never found wrong.
- **Population in Book One (1143): none.** Nobody lives at Ossuary Gate.
- **Frostmark 1144:** the Lamplighters' Guild established a permanent station of **nine** Guild
  members under the arch, giving the reopening of the Spur as its reason. From that month the Gate
  has a resident population of nine, and a tender who makes ten.

## Mirrowmere

A lake lying **east of the Line, off the twenty-eighth waystone**. The article gives no distance
from the Line: the ways in are seasonal tracks, nobody has surveyed them, and the figures travellers
offer disagree by a factor of three. None is repeated here.

Beneath the lake lies the drowned town of **Old Mirrow**, which the water took in Emberwane 1098.
Its roofs can be walked on when the water drops far enough, which happens in **Deepwinter** in most
years. The drop came out of season in Dustfall 1144.

## Kesh Ithar — waystone 40

Southern terminus of the Line. An oasis that became a city.

- **Population:** 68,000
- **Government:** the **Nine Tables**, nine merchant seats, held for life, one vote each. The fourth
  seat is held by Tulvo Brann.
- **Waystone:** the fortieth and last stone stands in the middle of the **Glass Bazaar**, with market
  stalls built against it on all four sides, so that it now serves as a pillar holding up an awning.
- **Trade:** glass above everything, then salt, brass and carriage.

## On the waystones themselves

Forty stones, of a grey stone that matches no quarry on the continent, each cut with a number in a
notation the Concord adopted rather than invented.

**Who set them is not known.** Not the Syndicate, not the Tables, not the Guild. There is no record
of the Line older than the Concord of 1012, and the stones were there before it. Several traditions
exist. None is evidence, and this article does not repeat them.
