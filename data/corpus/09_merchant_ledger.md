# Ledger of Tulvo Brann

Kept at the Glass Bazaar, Kesh Ithar. Four seasons are reproduced here, from Dustfall 1143 to
Hollow 1144.

## How to read these tables

| Column | Means |
|---|---|
| **Goods** | the commodity, in the ledger's own names |
| **Units** | count of units: vials, blocks, blanks, baskets, jars, coils or skins |
| **Per unit** | price paid per unit, **in tenth-marks** |
| **Total** | units × per unit, **in tenth-marks** |
| **Stone** | the waystone at which the consignment was taken up |

A silver mark is ten tenth-marks. Every figure in this ledger is a whole number of tenth-marks;
Brann's house has never kept fractions, on the grounds that a fraction in a ledger is an argument
waiting to happen.

**No subtotals are entered.** Brann's clerks were required to add the columns themselves each
season, and the ledger preserves that discipline. Any seasonal or commodity total must be computed
from the rows.

## Standing prices

Over these four seasons Brann's prices per unit did not move:

| Goods | Per unit (tenth-marks) |
|---|---|
| water (skins) | 2 |
| glass vials | 3 |
| dried figs | 4 |
| cord (coils) | 7 |
| lamp oil (jars) | 9 |
| salt blocks | 11 |
| brass blanks | 45 |

This is unusual and Brann was asked about it. "A price that moves every season teaches a caravan
master to haggle," he said. "A price that never moves teaches him to come back."

## A note on the tithe, in Brann's hand

> The tithe is not in this ledger and never will be. It is the Reach's claim on the wagons, not mine
> on the goods. A man who mixes the two will one day pay the Syndicate twice and believe he has made
> a profit. Reckon it where it is reckoned: per wagon, per stone passed, first and last counted, at
> whatever rate is in force in the month you set out.
